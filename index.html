Games

Diana

Side-scrolling delivery roguelite · Solo project · In development

  • Godot 4.6
  • GDScript
  • ~2,700 LOC

You fly cargo down a hostile lane for a dispatch office that will not tell you what is in the crates. The twist is that surviving the run and delivering well are two different skills — and the game pays you for the second one.

The loop

Take a contract → fly the lane → get graded on arrival → spend seeds at the depot → slot new modules → take a harder contract. Fourteen contracts unlock one at a time, each trading better pay against faster fuel burn and a longer lane.

Design decisions I’d defend in an interview

LEVEL DESIGN
Authored chunks, not random spawners

The lane emits hand-built obstacle patterns one at a time on distance, rather than firing independent random spawners on timers. That is what gives a run a readable rhythm instead of uniform noise. Coins are the pathing language — they trace the line you are meant to fly, or bait you into a worse one. They are never scattered at random.

PACING
Forced breathers

Every third chunk is guaranteed to be difficulty zero — no hazards at all. Pressure needs somewhere to release, otherwise a long run reads as one flat wall of stress and players stop registering the spikes.

ECONOMY
One letter tells the whole story

Cargo degrades from hazard hits, hard bounces and sustained high-G flying. The delivery grade (S–F) drives both the payout share and the reputation gained — so the letter on the results screen is the entire outcome, not a label sitting next to a separate calculation. Coins are deliberately excluded from grade scaling: a battered run still keeps what it picked up.

PROGRESSION
Slot scarcity forces identity

Thirty-one permanent modules across eight tags — but only two bays to start, five at most, with extra bays priced steeply. You cannot own the board. Scarcity is what turns a shopping list into a build.

SHOP
Five offers, not a catalogue

The depot shows a fixed five offers that rotate after each run, with a rising reroll cost. You meet the catalogue a few items at a time, so buying one is a real choice under uncertainty rather than optimisation against a full menu.

DIFFICULTY
A curve with no plateau

Scroll speed ramps fast, eases into a shoulder that keeps the opening readable, then creeps upward forever. Every run ends because the lane eventually outruns you — there is no speed you can settle into.

UI
One colour language everywhere

Blue always means one-use, purple always means permanent — in the shop, in the loadout screen and on the in-flight hotbar. The hotbar keys live in a single constant so three separate screens can never disagree about which key fires which slot.

FEEDBACK
Modules that show their work

Each slotted module gets a chip in the HUD, dim until it actually does something. Always-on modules stay lit; triggered ones flash on fire. You can see your build working instead of trusting a tooltip.
